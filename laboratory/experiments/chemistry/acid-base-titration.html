Determination of the concentration of hydrochloric acid using standard sodium hydroxide solution.

1. Aim

To determine the concentration of the given hydrochloric acid (HCl) solution by titrating it against a standard sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution using phenolphthalein indicator.

2. Apparatus Required

  • Burette
  • Volumetric pipette
  • Conical flask
  • Burette stand
  • Funnel
  • White tile
  • Beaker and wash bottle

3. Chemicals Required

  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl) solution – unknown concentration
  • Standard sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution
  • Phenolphthalein indicator
  • Distilled water

4. Theory

Acid-base titration is a quantitative analytical technique used to determine the unknown concentration of an acid or base by reacting it with a solution of known concentration.

In this experiment, hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide to form sodium chloride and water. This is a neutralization reaction.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Phenolphthalein is used as an indicator. The solution changes from colourless to a faint permanent pink at the endpoint when NaOH is added to the acid.

5. Formula

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂

Where:

  • M₁ = Molarity of HCl
  • V₁ = Volume of HCl
  • M₂ = Molarity of NaOH
  • V₂ = Volume of NaOH used

This formula applies because HCl and NaOH react in a 1:1 mole ratio.

6. Procedure

  1. Wash the burette with distilled water and rinse it with standard NaOH solution.
  2. Fill the burette with standard NaOH solution and note the initial reading.
  3. Wash the pipette with the given HCl solution.
  4. Transfer 20 ml of HCl into a clean conical flask using the pipette.
  5. Add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator.
  6. Add NaOH from the burette slowly while continuously swirling the flask.
  7. Stop when a faint pink colour persists for about 30 seconds.
  8. Note the final burette reading and repeat to obtain concordant readings.

8. Calculation

Suppose the following values are used:

  • Volume of HCl = 20 ml
  • Molarity of NaOH = 0.1 M
  • Average volume of NaOH = 19.77 ml
M₁ × 20 = 0.1 × 19.77

M₁ = (0.1 × 19.77) / 20

M₁ = 0.09885 M

Therefore, the illustrative concentration of HCl is approximately 0.09885 M.

9. Result

The concentration of the given hydrochloric acid solution is determined by acid-base titration using standard sodium hydroxide solution.

The final numerical result must be calculated using the actual concordant readings obtained in the laboratory.

10. Precautions

  • Ensure that there are no air bubbles in the burette tip.
  • Read the burette at eye level.
  • Add NaOH dropwise near the endpoint.
  • Use only 2–3 drops of indicator.
  • Do not overshoot the endpoint.
  • Wear safety goggles and gloves while handling chemicals.

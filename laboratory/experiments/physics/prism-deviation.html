1. Aim

To study refraction of light through a triangular glass prism and determine the angle of deviation for different angles of incidence.

2. Apparatus Required

  • Triangular glass prism
  • Ray box or narrow light source
  • White sheet of paper
  • Drawing board and pins
  • Protractor and ruler

3. Theory

When a light ray passes through a glass prism, it refracts at the first surface, travels inside the prism and refracts again at the second surface. The emergent ray is generally deviated from its original direction.

4. Formula

r₁ + r₂ = A
δ = i + e − A
sin(i) / sin(r₁) = n

Here i is incidence, e is emergence, A is prism angle, δ is deviation and n is refractive index (air assumed to have refractive index 1).

5. Procedure

  1. Place the prism on a sheet and trace its outline.
  2. Draw an incident ray at a selected angle.
  3. Allow the ray to pass through the prism.
  4. Mark the emergent ray and measure its angle.
  5. Repeat for different angles of incidence.
  6. Calculate the angle of deviation for each observation.

6. Important Observation

As the angle of incidence changes, the angle of deviation also changes. For a given prism and wavelength, the deviation reaches a minimum at the condition of minimum deviation.

At minimum deviation: i = e
n = sin[(A + δₘ) / 2] / sin(A / 2)

The second formula applies at the minimum-deviation condition.

10. Precautions

  1. Use a narrow and well-defined light ray.
  2. Keep the prism clean and stable.
  3. Measure angles from the normal, not the surface.
  4. Avoid parallax error while reading the protractor.
  5. Repeat observations for better accuracy.

1. Aim

To determine the acceleration due to gravity using a simple pendulum and to study the relationship between the length and time period of a simple pendulum.

2. Apparatus Required

  • Rigid retort stand and clamp
  • Light, inextensible thread
  • Spherical metallic bob
  • Metre scale
  • Stopwatch
  • Protractor

3. Theory

A simple pendulum consists of a small heavy bob suspended from a fixed support by a light inextensible thread. When displaced slightly from its mean position and released, it performs periodic oscillations.

For small angular displacements, the motion is approximately simple harmonic.

4. Formula

T = 2π √(L / g)
g = 4π²L / T²

L = effective length in metres; T = time period in seconds; g = acceleration due to gravity.

5. Procedure

  1. Measure the length from the point of suspension to the centre of the bob.
  2. Displace the bob through a small angle.
  3. Release it gently without pushing.
  4. Measure the time taken for a selected number of complete oscillations.
  5. Repeat the measurement and calculate the mean time period.
  6. Use the measured length and period to calculate g.

10. Precautions

  1. The point of suspension should be rigid.
  2. The thread should be light and inextensible.
  3. The bob should be released gently without a push.
  4. The angular displacement should be small.
  5. Measure the length up to the centre of the bob.
  6. Take repeated readings to reduce random error.

Educational simulation. The ideal theoretical period assumes small oscillations and negligible air resistance. Actual experimental readings may differ.
